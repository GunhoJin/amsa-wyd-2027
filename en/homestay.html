No special facilities or perfect home are needed — a simple place to sleep and a light breakfast are enough.

Full host FAQ (Korean) FAQ

Who can host

Based on the parish manual

PARISH

An active parishioner household

A household registered in our parish, with parish contributions and Easter or Christmas confession in the last three years. An adult must live in the home.

ROOM

Minimal sleeping space

No bed is needed. A 2 m × 1.5 m space per person for a sleeping bag and luggage is enough. Bathrooms can be shared with the family.

BREAKFAST

A light breakfast

Toast, cereal and milk are plenty. If that is hard, guests can join the parish breakfast by prior arrangement.

TWO OR MORE

Room for at least 2

Teen pilgrims always travel with an adult leader, so each home must be able to welcome two or more.

ACCESS

Getting to the parish and venues

Guests must be able to reach the parish and venues on foot or by public transport; if that is difficult, the host should be able to help.

Pilgrims bring their own sleeping bags and personal items. The pastor selects the final host families after reviewing applications.

Homestay schedule

2027.8.2 – 8.8 · subject to change

  1. 8.2 (Mon)

    Welcome

    After the parish welcome and orientation, meet your assigned pilgrims and take them home. Groups may arrive at different times.

  2. 8.3 – 8.6

    Living together

    Serve a light breakfast and go to the parish together. Pilgrims join WYD events during the day; in the evening, meet at the parish at the agreed time (around 5 pm) and go home together.

  3. 8.7 (Sat)

    Vigil

    Serve breakfast and see them off. Pilgrims stay at the Closing Mass venue after the Vigil, so they do not come back that night.

  4. 8.8 (Sun)

    Home after the Closing Mass

    No breakfast needed. When pilgrims return after the Closing Mass (around 2–4 pm), welcome them warmly so they can wash and rest.

  5. 8.9 (Mon)

    Farewell

    Serve breakfast, help with packing and say goodbye.

How guests are assigned

WISHES

Your preferences count

We ask in advance how many guests and which gender you prefer, and assign within what is comfortable for you.

SPACE

Separate spaces for men and women

Pilgrims of different sexes in the same home do not share a sleeping space.

LANGUAGE

Same language together

Where possible, pilgrims from the same country or language group are placed together to ease communication.

CARE

Special care

Minors, women, and pilgrims who need help with health or mobility are placed with suitable families under separate criteria.

Before you host

Covered at the preparatory meeting

WELCOME

Hospitality in faith

Lodging and meals are offered in a spirit of service. A greeting, a short introduction to Korean culture and a welcome sign are nice touches for the first meeting.

LIFE

Share house rules

Check allergies and other health needs in advance, and agree on house rules such as bedtime, showers and going out.

TALK

Communication tools

When words fail, use translation apps such as Papago or Google Translate and picture communication cards (AAC).

TOGETHER

Travel together, solve problems together

Hosts accompany guests between home and the parish. If a problem comes up, don’t handle it alone — tell the parish coordinator.

What the application asks

At church · office form

FAMILY

Family

Head of household details (name, baptismal name, phone, address) and household members

HOME

Home

Type of home, pets, languages spoken, preferred gender, number of guests, available space (e.g. 1 bed, sleeping-bag space)

CHECK

Confirmation

You confirm the homestay dates (8.2–8.8), a light breakfast, and that this is a faith activity shared with the community.

COST

Cost

There is no fee or payment. Families provide only sleeping space and a light breakfast; pilgrims pay for lunch, dinner and transport with a prepaid card.

Each parish of the Archdiocese of Seoul will host about 2,000 pilgrims, about 500 of them in homestays. Assignments are confirmed in July 2027. Source: WYD Seoul 2027 Organizing Committee, Parish Manual (April 2026) — subject to updates.

FAQ

Selected from the Amsa-dong Parish host FAQ

If you’re hesitating

I don’t speak English. Is that OK?

You can use translation apps and picture cards (AAC), and we try to place pilgrims who share a language.

My home is small and I have no spare bed.

No bed is needed. Pilgrims bring sleeping bags, mats and toiletries; room to lay out a sleeping bag per person is enough. Just make sure men and women don’t sleep in the same space.

I’m nervous about having strangers from abroad at home.

Only registered pilgrims whose identity is verified will stay; they wear name badges, and at least two stay together. Any problem is solved with the parish coordinator.

We both work, so no one is home during the day.

Pilgrims are out at WYD events during the day, so you don’t need to be home. Hosts accompany them to and from the parish morning and evening; if times don’t work, talk to the parish.

We’re an older couple. And we have pets.

Any household with an adult can apply. Note pets and type of home (apartment, villa, house) on the form and we will take them into account.

Eligibility · assignment

Can I apply if I’m registered in another parish?

You need to be registered in our parish, with parish contributions and Easter or Christmas confession in the last three years.

Why at least two guests?

Because teen pilgrims come with an adult leader.

Can I choose my guests’ nationality or gender?

Nationality can’t be chosen. Number of guests, preferred gender and languages noted on the form are respected as far as possible. Nationality and numbers are confirmed in July 2027.

How many pilgrims are coming to our parish?

About 2,000, of whom about 500 are expected to stay in homestays.

Daily life · meals

What should I serve for breakfast?

Toast, cereal and milk are fine; fruit or boiled eggs are a nice extra. If it’s difficult, arrange in advance for guests to use the parish breakfast.

Do I need to serve dinner?

No. If you like, you can share a meal such as the first evening’s dinner.

Do I need air conditioning or a separate bathroom?

The bathroom can be shared with the family. Without air conditioning, please provide a fan. It helps to show guests the Wi-Fi, power outlets and where to dry laundry.

How do I explain house rules?

On the first day, gently explain rules such as bedtime, showers, going out, shoes and recycling; a short note in English helps.

Safety · contact

What if a pilgrim gets sick or there’s a problem?

Don’t handle it alone — tell the parish coordinator right away. Treatment must be at a hospital designated by the organizing committee for insurance to apply.

Can I help in other ways besides hosting?

Yes — as a volunteer for guiding, lodging, meals, interpreting or admin. Apply on the Get Involved page.
